Curated list of London restaurants, cafes, pubs, and street food options (classic, trendy, local favorites) for a 5-day visit in April 2026. Organized by type and meal with brief notes on price, vibe, and reservations.

CENTRAL LONDON CLASSICS & MODERN BRITISH
- Rules (Covent Garden) – London’s oldest restaurant; traditional British game and puddings in historic wood‑panelled rooms. Price: £££. Best for: long, classic dinner or Sunday lunch. Reservations: essential, book well ahead.
- Simpson’s in the Strand (The Strand) – Roast meats carved tableside, very old‑school feel. Price: £££. Best for: traditional roast lunch or early dinner. Reservations: recommended, especially weekends.
- The Wolseley (Piccadilly) – Grand European café‑brasserie famous for breakfast but good all day. Price: £££. Best for: breakfast (eggs, pastries), or elegant lunch. Reservations: strongly recommended for breakfast and dinner; some walk‑in at the bar.
- Dishoom (multiple branches – Covent Garden, King’s Cross, Shoreditch, Kensington, etc.) – Bombay‑style café, one of London’s most beloved restaurant groups. Price: ££. Best for: breakfast (bacon naan, chai), casual lunch or dinner. Reservations: most branches take partial bookings; at peak times expect a queue (they usually offer chai while you wait).
- St. John (Smithfield or Bread & Wine in Spitalfields) – Nose‑to‑tail British cooking, simple but excellent ingredients. Price: £££. Best for: adventurous lunch or dinner; very “London foodie” classic. Reservations: essential for the main Smithfield restaurant; Bread & Wine slightly easier but still book.

TRENDY & BUZZY SPOTS
- BAO (Soho, King’s Cross, Shoreditch, etc.) – Taiwanese buns and small plates in a minimalist setting. Price: ££. Best for: fun, casual lunch or dinner. Reservations: many branches accept bookings; Soho often has a queue for walk‑ins.
- Hopper’s (Soho, King’s Cross, Marylebone) – Sri Lankan and South Indian food (dosa, hoppers, curries) in a lively room. Price: ££. Best for: flavour‑packed lunch or dinner. Reservations: recommended; some walk‑in bar space depending on branch.
- Kiln (Soho) – Thai grill with counter seating and a no‑frills, high‑flavour menu. Price: ££. Best for: dinner; better for adults than young kids due to bar‑counter format. Reservations: limited; they keep space for walk‑ins, go early.
- Gloria / Ave Mario / Circolo Popolare (Big Mamma group in Shoreditch, Covent Garden, Fitzrovia) – Over‑the‑top Italian trattorias with huge pastas and desserts and Instagrammy interiors. Price: ££. Best for: lively dinner, group meals. Reservations: essential in the evening, book as soon as your dates are firm.
- Plates (Old Street) – Creative, plant‑based fine dining that earned attention as one of London’s top vegan restaurants. Price: £££. Best for: special‑occasion dinner, especially for vegetarians/vegans. Reservations: essential.
- Speedboat Bar (Chinatown & Notting Hill) – Thai dishes inspired by Bangkok’s Chinatown, loud, colourful, and fun; highlighted as one of Time Out’s top London restaurants and so popular they opened a Portobello Road branch. Price: ££. Best for: casual, late dinner with drinks. Reservations: highly recommended in the evening, though they may keep some counter space for walk‑ins.

NEIGHBOURHOOD & “LOCAL FAVOURITE” FEEL
- 40 Maltby Street (Bermondsey) – Natural‑wine bar with excellent seasonal small plates under the railway arches; loved by chefs and locals. Price: ££. Best for: relaxed dinner or long lunch, especially combined with Maltby Street Market. Reservations: historically walk‑in focused or limited booking; check and plan to go early if no reservations.
- Mauby (Brockley, south London) – West Indian‑inspired neighbourhood restaurant born from the Jerk Off BBQ pop‑up; praised for hearty, well‑priced food and great vibes. Price: ££. Best for: dinner away from tourist areas. Reservations: advisable, especially weekends.
- Casse‑Croûte (Bermondsey Street) – Tiny French bistro with a short daily‑changing menu; very atmospheric and local. Price: ££. Best for: cosy dinner date or leisurely lunch. Reservations: they don’t rely on online booking; call ahead, as seats are limited.
- Laphet (Bermondsey, Shoreditch, West End) – Burmese restaurant serving shareable plates; a favourite in local food‑neighbourhood write‑ups. Price: ££. Best for: dinner with friends; good for sharing lots of dishes. Reservations: recommended.
- Brunswick House (Vauxhall) – Creative, modern European food inside an old Georgian building full of antiques; considered one of Time Out’s top spots. Price: £££. Best for: special but not stuffy dinner or anniversary‑type meal. Reservations: recommended.

CAFÉS, BAKERIES & BREAKFAST/BRUNCH
- The Wolseley (mentioned above) – Ideal for a classic sit‑down breakfast with table service.
- Dishoom (mentioned above) – Famous for breakfast naan rolls and chai; arrive early for shorter waits.
- Granger & Co (Notting Hill, King’s Cross, Chelsea) – Australian‑style café with excellent brunch (ricotta hotcakes, scrambled eggs) and good coffee. Price: ££. Best for: brunch or relaxed lunch. Reservations: limited; many branches are mostly walk‑in with queues at peak times.
- Gail’s Bakery (multiple locations) – Reliable chain bakery for excellent pastries, sandwiches, and coffee. Price: £. Best for: quick breakfast near your accommodation. Reservations: not needed.
- Regency Café (Pimlico/Westminster) – Classic, no‑frills “caff” serving full English breakfasts; famous from film shoots. Cash or card policies may vary. Price: £. Best for: hearty, very British breakfast. Reservations: no, it’s counter‑service; be ready to queue at peak times.
- E5 Bakehouse (London Fields) – Artisanal bakery and café focusing on sourdough and seasonal lunches. Price: £. Best for: casual breakfast or light lunch, especially if exploring east London. Reservations: not taken; order at the counter.

PUBS & GASTROPUBS
- The Devonshire (Soho) – New‑school pub known for seriously good food from its in‑house butcher and bakery; singled out as a must‑book spot for roasts. Price: £££ for the restaurant upstairs, ££ in the pub. Best for: Sunday roast or proper British meat‑focused dinner. Reservations: essential for the dining room; bar downstairs more casual but very busy.
- The Harwood Arms (Fulham) – Michelin‑starred gastropub with refined British game and seasonal dishes. Price: £££. Best for: elevated lunch or dinner in a more residential area. Reservations: essential.
- The Churchill Arms (Kensington) – Famous flower‑covered exterior and very photogenic; Thai food served inside. Price: ££. Best for: a pint and casual meal after sightseeing in Kensington. Reservations: usually not for the pub area; expect it to be busy.
- The Pelican (Notting Hill) – Revamped historic pub with excellent elevated pub food; regularly mentioned in London best‑of lists. Price: ££. Best for: cosy dinner or Sunday lunch near Portobello Road. Reservations: recommended for sit‑down meals.

MARKETS & STREET FOOD
- Borough Market (London Bridge) – London’s most famous food market, packed with traders selling British and international foods (excellent for grazing on cheese, charcuterie, baked goods, and street‑food stands). Price: £‑££ depending how much you sample. Best for: lunch; go before or after visiting nearby sights like Southwark Cathedral or the Globe. Reservations: not applicable, but it gets crowded – weekdays or early mornings are calmer.
- Maltby Street Market (Bermondsey, weekends) – Smaller, more local‑feeling weekend market under the arches with great coffee, baked goods, and street‑food stalls. Price: £. Best for: informal brunch or lunch, especially combined with 40 Maltby Street. Reservations: not needed.
- Seven Dials Market (Covent Garden) – Indoor food hall with multiple vendors (great if your group wants different cuisines). Price: ££. Best for: casual lunch or relaxed early dinner around West End theatre plans. Reservations: generally walk‑in; some larger groups can book.
- Camden Market (Camden Town) – Huge collection of stalls with global street food; good option before or after a canal walk or gig. Price: £. Best for: casual lunch, especially if you like lots of choice. Reservations: not required.
- Mercato Metropolitano (Elephant & Castle) – Large, community‑minded food hall with many street‑food kiosks and bars plus frequent events. Price: ££. Best for: casual dinner or drinks with friends. Reservations: usually walk‑in; some individual traders or areas may offer bookings.

HOW TO USE THIS FOR A 5‑DAY TRIP
- Breakfast ideas: Gail’s Bakery or similar near your hotel most days; 1–2 “big” breakfasts at The Wolseley, Dishoom, or Regency Café.
- Lunch ideas: markets (Borough, Maltby, Camden, Seven Dials), plus a sit‑down classic like Simpson’s or a trendy spot such as BAO or Hopper’s on a museum/central London day.
- Dinner ideas: mix of one or two classics (Rules, St. John), one trendy buzzy place (Speedboat Bar, Kiln, Big Mamma group), one neighbourhood favourite (40 Maltby Street, Mauby, Casse‑Croûte), and one pub or gastropub (The Devonshire, The Pelican, Harwood Arms).

RESERVATION & BUDGET GUIDANCE
- Reservation‑heavy places (book as early as you know your dates): The Wolseley, Rules, Simpson’s in the Strand, St. John, Hopper’s (popular branches), Kiln (if they allow bookings on your dates), Big Mamma group restaurants, Plates, Brunswick House, The Devonshire (dining room), The Harwood Arms.
- Easier or walk‑in‑friendly: Dishoom (but expect queues at peak times), BAO (depending on branch), many cafés and bakeries (Gail’s, E5, Regency Café), markets and food halls, The Churchill Arms, many pub bars.
- Budget shorthand (per person, food only): £ = under ~£15, ££ = ~£15‑35, £££ = £35‑70+.

All of these are established or widely recommended as of 2024‑2025. Always double‑check current opening hours and booking policies closer to April 2026, as London’s restaurant scene changes quickly.
